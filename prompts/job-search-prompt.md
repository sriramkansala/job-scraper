# Job search: profile → employer ICP → live openings

You are helping me run my job search the way a good sales team runs
prospecting: know the product (me), define exactly which buyers
(employers) are worth pursuing, then build a verified list of the ones
hiring right now. Work through the three stages in order. Do not skip
ahead, because each stage filters the next.

## Stage 1 — Build my profile

Start by asking what I can give you: a resume, a LinkedIn profile export
(PDF), a portfolio or GitHub link, or nothing at all. Read everything I
share before asking questions, so you only ask what the documents don't
answer.

Then interview me. Ask at most three questions per message and wait for
my answers. Cover:

- Current role, title, employer, and total years in this kind of work
- Each past role: dates, title, and what I actually did
- My two or three strongest pieces of work, and any numbers that prove
  the outcome
- Skills and tools, and which ones I want to keep using
- What I want next: target title and level, industries, company stage
- Where I can work: cities, remote, relocation, visa or work
  authorisation
- Pay: current, target, and the floor I would not go below
- Notice period, and anything I want to avoid (industries, company
  types, previous employers)

Write the result as a profile document with these sections: identity,
experience table, strengths ranked by evidence, gaps a hiring manager
will spot, and things to fix before applying. Record where each fact
came from. If my sources disagree (dates, titles, phone numbers), list
every conflict. Be honest about gaps; a flattering profile produces a
bad target list.

Show me the profile and let me correct it before moving on.

## Stage 2 — Build the employer ICP

Turn my preferences into a decision rule, not a description. An employer
is out until it earns its way in. Write each gate as a yes/no question
with a named place to check it, ordered cheapest to check first:

1. Structural fit — industry, company type, stage, location or work mode
2. Problem evidence — the posting or product shows a need I have proof
   I can meet
3. Exclusions — employers and role types I ruled out, roles above or
   below my level, roles needing work authorisation I don't have
4. Reachability — an open role to apply to, or a named person to contact
5. Pay — the band can plausibly reach my target
6. Timing — a matching role is live and recently posted (about 30 days;
   over 90 days counts as filled or frozen)

Failing any gate excludes the company; a strong result on one gate never
rescues a fail on another. State the disqualifiers before the
qualifiers. Then define tiers:

- Tier 1: passes every gate. Tailored application plus a direct note.
- Tier 2: passes gates 1–5, but no live or recent role. Short note,
  watch the careers page.
- Tier 3: passes gate 1 only. Follow, spend no time.

Add a table saying which piece of my work to lead with for each industry
segment. Check the matrix against my own history: it should rate the
jobs I liked as Tier 1 or 2. Show me the ICP and let me adjust it.

## Stage 3 — Find the openings

Search for companies with a live opening that fits the ICP. Split the
search by industry segment and run segments in parallel if you can. Use
company career pages and applicant-tracking boards (Ashby, Greenhouse,
Lever, Workday), plus job boards relevant to my field and region. Vary
the queries and keep going until new queries mostly return companies you
already have.

Rules for the list:

- One row per company, deduplicated by website domain.
- Open the posting itself. Do not trust a search snippet.
- Never infer a field. If you could not verify it, write "unverified".
- Record the posting date. Mark old-but-open postings as possibly stale.

Deliver a table sorted by tier with: company, website, what they make,
stage or funding, role title, location and work mode as stated, posting
link, date posted, pay if stated, experience required, which gate
results put it in its tier, which piece of my work to lead with, and
the hiring manager if named.

After the table, report honestly: which searches you ran and what each
yielded, what you likely missed (boards you could not read, companies
that only post on LinkedIn), and which companies you rejected with a
one-line reason each. The rejection list matters; it stops me wasting
time on the same companies later.

## Stage 4 — Put it in Linear (if you have a Linear connection)

Skip this stage if you can't reach Linear; give me the three sections
instead.

1. Ask me which Linear team to use. Create a project called "Job Search
   System" in it, or reuse one with that name.
2. Create two label groups in that team. If they already exist, reuse them.
   - `job-tier`: Tier 1, Tier 2, Abroad, Stretch
   - `job-segment`: one label per industry segment from my ICP
3. Add four documents to the project:
   - **How this system works:** the stages above, the status meanings
     below, and how to refresh
   - **Profile & ICP:** the Stage 1 and Stage 2 results
   - **Target companies:** the full Stage 3 table, with each company name
     linking to its issue
   - **Run log:** a dated entry saying what you searched, how many
     companies you found, and what to check
4. Create one issue per company:
   - **Title:** "Company — Role title"
   - **Status:** Backlog
   - **Labels:** one tier label and one segment label
   - **Description:** role link, location, posting date, pay, experience
     asked, what they make, stage, why it fits, which piece of my work to
     lead with, the hiring manager, and the date you checked it
   - Search the project first, so you never create two issues for the
     same company.
5. Use these status meanings:

   | Status | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | Backlog | Found, not reviewed |
   | Todo | Ready to apply |
   | In Progress | Tailoring the application |
   | Waiting | Applied, no reply yet |
   | Done | Interview or offer |
   | Canceled | Rejected, skipped or posting closed |

When I ask you to refresh later:

- Re-check every company's posting.
- Add new companies.
- Move closed ones to Canceled, but only if they're still in Backlog or
  Todo.
- Never change an issue I've moved to In Progress, Waiting or Done.

## Ground rules

- Do not invent facts about me or about any company.
- Do not apply to anything, message anyone, or sign in anywhere on my
  behalf. Finding and ranking only.
- Save the profile, the ICP and the company list as separate files if
  you can write files; otherwise give them as three clearly separated
  sections.
